# Genre profiles

Use the author's draft, voice sample, audience, and publication constraints to select a register.
Keep distinctive wording that conveys a claim or helps the reader.

| Genre | Look for | Edit |
|---|---|---|
| Academic paper | Unsupported novelty or significance claims such as “crucial implications.” | State the finding, effect size, limitations, and citations when available. Keep the venue's conventions. |
| Short social post | “Let that sink in,” an engagement question, or a setup longer than the claim. | Lead with the claim. Keep the author's capitalization and respect the platform limit. |
| Forum post or comment | A long preamble or stock closing on a short answer. | Answer directly. Keep formatting that aids scanning. Include firsthand details only if the author supplied them. |
| LinkedIn post | One-line paragraphs that repeat a conclusion, or a generic “Agree?” | Name the event, decision, or result. State its consequence once. |
| Email | A restatement of the recipient's question or a formulaic greeting. | Answer the question early. Match the thread's greeting, formality, and sign-off. |
| Essay or newsletter | A preview and conclusion that repeat the same argument. | Open with a documented scene, number, or claim. Keep headings where readers need navigation. |
| Marketing copy | “Seamless,” “game-changing,” or a benefit without a mechanism. | Name the user, benefit, and mechanism. Use a number only when the source supports it. |
| Technical documentation | Unmeasured performance claims or “simply” before an unfamiliar step. | Give the command, required version, and expected result. Keep definitions and lists when they help lookup. |
| Cover letter, statement, or grant | “Passionate” or “unique perspective” without evidence. | Describe a real decision, contribution, or result. Preserve appropriate formality. |

Read the edited passage against its intended use. Keep genuine uncertainty, counterarguments, and the author's position.
